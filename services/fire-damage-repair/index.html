Fire Damage Repair & Rebuild in Nashville, TN

Quick answer: Fire damage repair in Nashville, TN rebuilds what the fire and cleanup removed: framing, roof decking, drywall, insulation, electrical, plumbing, cabinets and finishes. In Davidson County, Metro Codes requires permits for work such as wall removal, drywall repair over 100 square feet and electrical or plumbing changes, and Tennessee licensing rules apply based on the project's value.

Repairs are the last and often longest stage of fire damage restoration in Nashville. They turn a gutted room back into a kitchen or bedroom. A provider reached through Nashville Fire Removal Pros can scope the rebuild, explain the permits involved and work with your adjuster on the estimate.

What fire damage repair includes

  • A written scope of work that lists every repair, material and quantity.
  • Structural repairs to joists, studs, rafters and roof decking weakened by fire.
  • New insulation, drywall, trim and paint in affected rooms.
  • Roofing and siding repairs where the fire or firefighters opened the exterior.
  • Electrical, plumbing and HVAC repairs by trade professionals with the required state or local licenses.
  • Cabinets, countertops, flooring and fixtures matched as closely as possible to the originals.
  • Permit applications and inspections with Metro Codes or the local city.
  • A final walk-through against the scope before the job is closed.

The fire damage repair process

  1. Scope and estimate. The provider measures the damage and writes a line-item scope for you and your adjuster.
  2. Insurance review. You, the adjuster and the provider agree on the scope. Hidden damage found later is reported for a supplement.
  3. Permits. The provider applies for building and trade permits with Metro Codes or the city.
  4. Structural work. Framing, roof decking and subfloors are repaired or replaced.
  5. Rough-in and inspections. Electrical, plumbing and HVAC work is completed and inspected before walls close.
  6. Insulation and drywall. Walls and ceilings are insulated, hung, finished and primed with a sealing primer.
  7. Finishes. Cabinets, flooring, trim, paint and fixtures go in.
  8. Final inspection and walk-through. Permits are closed, and you review the work against the scope.

Tennessee licensing and Nashville permits

The Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors sets licensing by project value. A home improvement license is required in Davidson, Rutherford and seven other counties for residential remodeling projects above $3,000 and under $25,000. A contractor license is required anywhere in Tennessee for projects of $25,000 or more, counting materials, labor and profit. Electrical and plumbing work have their own license rules. Williamson and Sumner counties are not on the home improvement license list, so the state contractor threshold and local rules apply there.

Metro Nashville Codes requires a permit for interior work such as new or relocated doors, ceilings or stairs, wall removal, drywall repair or replacement over 100 square feet, changes to window size, and relocating electrical or plumbing fixtures. Interior painting and floor laying generally do not need a permit. Owners who live in the home can pull their own permit, but most fire rebuilds are handled by the contractor. Franklin, Brentwood, Hendersonville, Goodlettsville and Murfreesboro run their own building departments.

What affects the cost of fire repairs

The biggest factors are structural damage, the square footage being rebuilt, roofing needs, electrical and plumbing replacement, material choices, code upgrades required by current building codes, and permit fees. Older homes may need upgrades to wiring or smoke alarms that the original house did not have. Ask whether your policy includes ordinance or law coverage, which can help pay for required code upgrades.

DIY vs. hiring a contractor

Owners sometimes handle painting or simple trim work themselves to save money. Structural repairs, roofing, electrical and plumbing should be done by qualified trades who hold the required state license, with permits. Unpermitted work can cause problems with your insurer, with resale and with your own safety. If you are thinking about selling instead of rebuilding, read how to sell a fire-damaged house in Nashville.

Insurance notes for Tennessee

Structure payments may be made out to you and your mortgage company. That can hold funds in escrow and release them as repairs are completed. If the contractor finds hidden damage, the Tennessee Department of Commerce & Insurance advises notifying your insurer so the adjuster and contractor can review it together. Landlords should also read the landlord insurance and fire damage guide.

Local note: Rebuilds in historic parts of Franklin and East Nashville and Inglewood may need extra care to match original trim and siding, and some properties sit in historic overlay districts with design review. Newer subdivisions in Murfreesboro usually rebuild faster because materials match current stock.

Fire damage repair questions

Do I need a permit to repair fire damage in Nashville?

Usually. Metro Codes requires permits for structural work, wall removal, drywall over 100 square feet, roofing over one-third of the roof and electrical or plumbing changes. The provider can confirm which permits your job needs.

What license should a fire repair contractor have in Tennessee?

For projects of $25,000 or more, a Tennessee contractor license. In Davidson and Rutherford counties, remodeling projects above $3,000 and under $25,000 need a home improvement license. Ask for the license number and verify it with the state.

Can I choose my own contractor, or must I use the insurer's?

In most cases you can choose your own contractor. Your insurer may suggest providers, but you can get your own estimate and share it with the adjuster.

How long does a fire rebuild take?

A single-room repair may take a few weeks. A larger rebuild with structural work, permits and inspections often takes three to six months or more.

Will my insurance pay for code upgrades?

Only if your policy includes ordinance or law coverage, or the upgrade falls within your dwelling limit. Check your declarations page and ask your agent.
