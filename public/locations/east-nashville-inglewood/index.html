Why fire damage in East Nashville is different

East Nashville has a long history with disaster. The Great East Nashville Fire of 1916 burned hundreds of homes. The 1998 Nashville tornado damaged much of the area, and the March 2020 tornado tore through Five Points and surrounding blocks. The 2010 flood also affected parts of the area. Many homes have been repaired or rebuilt more than once, so old and new materials often sit side by side.

Older houses here often have balloon framing, where wall cavities run from the foundation to the attic without fire blocking. Fire and smoke can travel up inside these walls and appear far from where they started. Plaster and lath walls, heart-pine floors and original windows can often be saved. But they need careful cleaning and repair rather than quick replacement.

Inglewood has more 1940s and 1950s brick and frame houses, many with original or partly updated wiring, crawlspaces and small attics. New infill homes, often tall and narrow and built close to older neighbors, add another mix. Houses on small lots sit close together, so a fire next door can crack windows, melt siding and push smoke inside.

Tips for East Nashville and Inglewood homeowners

  • Older wiring: If your home still has knob-and-tube or ungrounded circuits, have a qualified electrician inspect it before and after any fire repair.
  • Original materials: Photograph mantels, trim, doors and hardware before cleanup so they can be saved or matched.
  • Historic overlays: Edgefield and several other neighborhoods have historic or conservation overlays. So exterior changes may need Metro Historic Zoning Commission review.
  • Neighbor fires: If a fire next door damaged your siding or windows, photograph it right away and file with your own insurer.
  • Crawlspaces: Check crawlspaces for standing water within a day of the fire to reduce mold risk.

Permits and historic review in East Nashville

East Nashville and Inglewood are inside Metro Nashville, so permits come from Metro Codes. Wall removal, drywall replacement over 100 square feet, electrical or plumbing relocation and roofing over one-third of the roof all need permits. Homes inside a historic or conservation overlay may also need review before exterior work. Davidson County requires a home improvement license for residential projects above $3,000 and under $25,000, and a state contractor license for larger jobs.

Fire damage restoration in East Nashville and Inglewood questions

Do providers cover all of East Nashville and Inglewood?

Yes. Providers in this network serve zip codes 37206 and 37216, including Edgefield, Lockeland Springs, Five Points, Cleveland Park, Eastwood, Rosebank and Inglewood.

Can plaster walls be saved after a fire?

Often. Plaster with light smoke damage can usually be cleaned and sealed. Plaster that is soaked, cracked or charred may need repair or replacement in sections.

Do I need historic review for fire repairs?

If your home is inside a historic or conservation overlay, exterior changes may need Metro Historic Zoning Commission review. Interior repairs usually do not.

Why did smoke appear upstairs when the fire was in the basement?

In balloon-framed houses, open wall cavities let smoke and fire travel from floor to floor. Walls may need to be opened to check for hidden damage.

My neighbor's fire damaged my house. What do I do?

Photograph the damage, call your own insurer and let it decide whether to seek repayment from the neighbor's insurer.
