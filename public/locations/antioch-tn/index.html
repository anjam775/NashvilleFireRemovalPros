Why fire damage in Antioch is different

A large share of Antioch housing is multifamily. Apartment complexes built from the 1970s through the 2000s line Bell Road, Murfreesboro Pike, Hickory Hollow Parkway and Nolensville Pike. In these buildings, one kitchen fire can send smoke through shared attics and hallways and send firefighting water into units below. Several households may need help at once, each with its own insurance.

Townhome communities raise similar questions. The HOA master policy may cover the roof, exterior walls and shared structure, while the owner's policy covers interiors and contents. Renters need their own policy for belongings and temporary housing.

Single-family homes in Cane Ridge and near Percy Priest Lake are often newer construction with vinyl siding and attached garages. Burned plastics leave sticky residue that smears easily, so soot removal needs the right cleaners.

Homes near Percy Priest Lake and along the creeks that run through Antioch also deal with Middle Tennessee humidity. Firefighting water left in drywall, carpet pads and crawlspaces can start growing mold within a day or two in warm weather. So drying should begin as soon as the fire department releases the property.

Tips for Antioch residents

  • Renters: Buy renters insurance. It is usually inexpensive and covers belongings, temporary housing and liability. The renters insurance guide explains what it pays for.
  • Townhome owners: Ask your HOA for a copy of its master policy so you know where its coverage ends and yours begins.
  • Smoke alarms: Through the Get Alarmed, Tennessee! program, many local fire departments install free smoke alarms. Contact the Nashville Fire Department to ask.
  • Close the door: Closing doors behind you when you leave a fire slows smoke and flames, according to Tennessee fire safety guidance.
  • Photos first: Photograph damage in your unit and any shared areas before cleanup starts.

Permits and licensing in Antioch

Because Antioch is part of Metro Nashville, permits come from Metro Codes. Metro requires permits for work such as removing walls, replacing more than 100 square feet of drywall, changing doors, ceilings or stairs, relocating electrical or plumbing, and replacing more than one-third of a roof. Painting, flooring and insulation generally do not need a permit. Davidson County is one of the nine Tennessee counties that require a home improvement license for residential projects above $3,000 and under $25,000, and larger projects need a state contractor license.

Fire damage restoration in Antioch questions

Do providers cover all of Antioch?

Yes. Providers in this network serve all of Antioch in zip code 37013, including Bell Road, Hickory Hollow, Cane Ridge, Murfreesboro Pike, Nolensville Pike and areas near Percy Priest Lake.

Is Antioch part of Nashville for permits?

Yes. Antioch is inside Metro Nashville and Davidson County, so building permits come from Metro Codes.

Who pays when a fire starts in a neighbor's apartment?

The landlord's policy usually covers the building. Your renters policy covers your belongings and temporary housing. Your insurer may seek repayment from the responsible party.

My townhome was damaged. Do I call the HOA or my insurer?

Both. The HOA master policy often covers the structure and exterior, while your unit policy covers interiors and contents.

What should I do first after an apartment fire?

Make sure everyone is safe, follow fire department instructions, photograph the damage, tell your landlord and call your renters insurer.
