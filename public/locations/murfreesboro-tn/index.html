Why fire damage in Murfreesboro is different

Middle Tennessee State University shapes a large part of the city. Thousands of students rent houses, duplexes and apartments around the campus, and many of those units are older homes divided into rentals or 1980s and 1990s apartment complexes. Kitchen fires are common in rentals, and NFPA reports that cooking causes a much larger share of fires in apartments than in single-family homes.

Murfreesboro has also been one of the fastest-growing cities in Tennessee since the 1990s. New subdivisions in the Blackman, Barfield and Christiana areas are full of homes with vinyl siding, engineered floor joists and attached garages. These materials burn and melt differently than older wood construction. Smoke from burning plastics leaves sticky, wet residue that smears easily and needs special cleaners.

Downtown, around the historic Rutherford County Courthouse and the Murfree Spring area, older brick and frame houses have their own needs, from plaster walls to older electrical systems.

Murfreesboro's growth also means many homeowners are new to the area and to their insurance policies. Reviewing dwelling limits after a home's value rises, and keeping a phone inventory of belongings, makes a fire claim far simpler. Renters near campus often share a unit, so each roommate needs their own renters policy.

Tips for Murfreesboro residents

  • Renters: Your landlord's insurance usually covers the building, not your belongings. Read the renters insurance and fire damage guide and keep a phone inventory of what you own.
  • Landlords: Document each unit's condition at move-in so a fire claim can show pre-loss condition. The landlord insurance guide covers lost rent and liability.
  • New homeowners: Clean dryer lint filters after every load and check the vent run once a year.
  • Townhome owners: Ask your HOA how its master policy and your unit policy split responsibility for shared walls and roofs.
  • Everyone: Never leave cooking unattended. NFPA identifies unattended cooking as the leading factor in home cooking fires.

Permits and licensing in Murfreesboro

The City of Murfreesboro runs its own building and codes department. So permits for fire repairs inside city limits go through the city rather than Metro Nashville. Rutherford County is one of the nine Tennessee counties that require a home improvement license for residential remodeling projects above $3,000 and under $25,000. Projects of $25,000 or more require a state contractor license. Ask any provider for the license number that fits your job, and verify it with the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors.

Fire damage restoration in Murfreesboro questions

Do providers cover all of Murfreesboro?

Yes. Providers in this network serve Murfreesboro zip codes 37127, 37128, 37129, 37130 and 37132, including the MTSU area, downtown, Blackman, Barfield and neighborhoods along Medical Center Parkway.

Who pays for fire damage in a rental?

Usually the landlord's policy pays to repair the building, while the tenant's renters policy pays for belongings and temporary housing. Responsibility can shift if someone's negligence caused the fire.

What license does a fire repair contractor need in Rutherford County?

A home improvement license for remodeling projects above $3,000 and under $25,000, and a state contractor license for projects of $25,000 or more.

Can smoke from a neighbor's unit damage my apartment?

Yes. Smoke travels through shared attics, walls and ventilation. Report it to your landlord and your renters insurer, and photograph any residue or odor.

How long does fire restoration take in an apartment?

A small kitchen fire may take one to two weeks. Damage spread across several units can take longer because of building-wide permits and insurance coordination.
