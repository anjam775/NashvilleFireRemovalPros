How to Sell a Fire-Damaged House in Nashville

By Nashville Fire Removal Pros Editorial · Published November 10, 2026 · Updated November 10, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: You can sell a fire-damaged house in Nashville as-is, repair it first and sell at full market value, or sell to a cash buyer or investor. Tennessee sellers must disclose known fire damage on the state disclosure form unless the buyer waives it in writing. Talk with your insurer before you list, because the sale can affect how your claim is paid.

Deciding what to do with a house after a fire is a big financial choice. Some owners want to rebuild and stay. Others would rather take the insurance money and move on. Nashville Fire Removal Pros can connect you with a local provider for fire damage repair in Nashville, and a repair estimate is useful even if you plan to sell. This is because it shows buyers and agents what the house needs.

Can you sell a fire-damaged house?

Yes. There is no rule against selling a house with fire damage. The real questions are how much you will get, how fast it will sell and what you must tell buyers. Your three main options are:

Option Speed Price Best when
Sell as-is on the open market Medium Lower than market value Damage is moderate and the location is strong
Repair first, then sell Slow Closest to full market value You have time and insurance money to rebuild
Sell to a cash buyer or investor Fast Usually the lowest offer You need a quick, simple sale

How to sell a fire-damaged house as-is

Selling as-is means the buyer accepts the house in its current condition. It saves you the work of rebuilding, but buyers will price in the repairs and the risk.

  1. Secure the property: Board up openings and tarp the roof so the damage does not grow and the house stays safe for showings.
  2. Get a repair estimate: A written scope from a restoration provider helps buyers understand the real cost.
  3. Clean up debris: Removing burned materials and smoke residue makes the house easier to show.
  4. Price for the damage: Ask an agent for comparable sales and subtract a realistic repair cost.
  5. Disclose the fire: Use Tennessee's disclosure form, or a disclaimer if the buyer waives disclosure.
  6. Expect cash or renovation loans: Many standard mortgages will not fund a house that is not livable.

Should you repair before selling?

Repairing first usually brings the highest price, but it takes the most time and effort. It makes sense when:

The insurance settlement will cover most of the rebuild. The house is in a strong neighborhood where finished homes sell quickly. You can wait several months for repairs and a sale. The damage is mostly smoke and cosmetic, not structural.

If the fire was minor, professional cleanup, soot removal and odor treatment may be enough to sell at close to full value.

Tennessee disclosure rules for fire damage

Tennessee's Residential Property Disclosure Act applies to most sales of homes with one to four units. The seller must give the buyer one of these before the purchase contract is accepted:

  • A disclosure statement listing known material defects. The state form asks directly about property or structural damage from fire, earthquake, floods or landslides.
  • A disclaimer statement saying the home is sold as-is with no representations. This is allowed only when the buyer waives the disclosure.

Some transfers are exempt under Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-5-209. Hiding known fire damage can expose a seller to claims for misrepresentation. When in doubt, disclose and keep records of repairs, permits and inspections.

How insurance affects the sale

Your insurance claim and your sale are connected. Before listing, ask your insurer:

  • Will they pay replacement cost if you do not rebuild? Many policies pay actual cash value first and release the rest only after repairs are made. Selling without rebuilding may mean you receive only the depreciated amount.
  • Who gets the structure payment? Tennessee's Department of Commerce and Insurance notes that structure checks may include your mortgage company, which must be paid off at closing.
  • Can the claim be assigned to the buyer? Some policies allow it, but many do not. Get it in writing.
  • What about contents and living expenses? These are usually paid to you regardless of the sale.

Selling a fire-damaged home in the Nashville market

Nashville's housing demand means fire-damaged homes in desirable areas can still attract buyers, especially investors and builders. In neighborhoods like East Nashville, where land value is a large part of the price, buyers may focus on the lot as much as the house.

Repairs in Nashville follow local rules. Metro Codes requires permits for structural repairs, larger drywall replacement, electrical and plumbing changes and major roofing work. Davidson County requires a home improvement license for residential projects above $3,000 and under $25,000, and a state contractor license for $25,000 or more. Buyers and their lenders often ask for permit records, so keep them.

How to protect your sale price

  • Act fast on drying: Mold growth after firefighting water makes a house much harder to sell.
  • Keep the house secure: Vandalism and theft add damage and liability.
  • Maintain insurance: Tell your insurer the home is vacant and ask about vacancy coverage limits.
  • Gather paperwork: Repair estimates, permits, inspection reports and the fire report reassure buyers.

Local note: The best choice depends on the area. Strong demand in East Nashville and Brentwood can support repairing first, fast-growing Murfreesboro attracts investors, and older homes in Goodlettsville and Madison may sell faster as-is to buyers who plan a full renovation.

Selling a fire-damaged house questions

Do I have to disclose a past fire when selling in Tennessee?

Generally yes, if you know about it. The Tennessee disclosure form asks about fire damage. Repaired damage should still be disclosed, along with records of the repairs.

How much value does a house lose after a fire?

It depends on the damage, the repair cost and the neighborhood. Buyers usually subtract repair costs plus a margin for risk and time.

Can I sell my house before the insurance claim is settled?

Often yes, but it can affect your payout. Ask your insurer how a sale changes replacement cost payments and who receives the structure check.

Will a buyer get a mortgage on a fire-damaged house?

Many standard loans require a livable home. Buyers often use cash or renovation loans for fire-damaged properties.

Is it better to rebuild or sell after a fire?

Rebuilding usually gets a higher price but takes months. Selling as-is or to an investor is faster but brings less money. A repair estimate helps you compare.
