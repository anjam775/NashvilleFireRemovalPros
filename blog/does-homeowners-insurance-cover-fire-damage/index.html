Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Fire Damage? What Nashville Owners Should Know

By Nashville Fire Removal Pros Editorial · Published November 10, 2026 · Updated November 10, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: Yes, homeowners insurance covers fire damage in almost every standard policy. An HO-3 policy pays to repair the house, other structures and your belongings, and covers extra living costs while you are displaced. The main exceptions are fires set on purpose, damage from long-term neglect and homes left vacant beyond the policy's limit.

When a house fire hits, insurance is often the first thing owners worry about after their family's safety. The good news is that fire is the most basic peril a homeowners policy covers. Does homeowners insurance cover fire damage?

Yes. A standard HO-3 policy has four main parts that respond to a fire:

Coverage What it pays for Fire example
Coverage A: Dwelling The house and attached structures Framing, roof, drywall, cabinets, attached garage
Coverage B: Other structures Detached buildings on the property Detached garage, shed, fence
Coverage C: Personal property Your belongings Furniture, clothing, electronics
Coverage D: Loss of use Extra living costs while the home is unlivable Hotel or rental, extra meals, mileage

Liability coverage (Coverage E) can also apply if a fire that started at your home damages a neighbor's property.

Replacement cost vs. actual cash value

Most dwelling coverage pays replacement cost, the cost to rebuild with similar materials. Personal property may pay actual cash value, which subtracts depreciation, unless you bought replacement cost coverage. Some insurers pay the depreciated amount first and release the rest after repairs are finished. Ask your adjuster how your policy handles this so you can plan your budget.

What homeowners insurance usually does not cover

  • Intentional fires: A fire set on purpose by an insured person is excluded.
  • Vacancy: Many policies limit fire coverage if the home has been vacant for more than a set period, often 60 consecutive days. Tell your insurer before leaving a house empty.
  • Neglect and wear: Damage from a known problem that was never fixed, like ignored wiring issues, can be disputed.
  • Limits and sub-limits: Coverage stops at your policy limits. Jewelry, art and collections often have low caps unless scheduled.
  • Code upgrades: Rebuilding to current code can cost more. Ordinance or law coverage pays for this, but it may be limited or optional.

How much will insurance pay after a house fire?

Insurance pays up to your limits, minus your deductible. The payout depends on the scope of damage the adjuster accepts, your coverage type and your limits. If your dwelling limit is too low for current building costs, you could be underinsured. Extended replacement cost coverage adds a cushion, often a percentage above the limit, for this reason.

Steps to take after a house fire

  1. Wait for clearance: Do not re-enter until the fire department says the home is safe.
  2. Report the claim: Call your insurer the same day and get a claim number.
  3. Document the damage: Take photos and video before anything is moved, as the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance advises.
  4. Prevent further damage: Board up openings, tarp the roof and remove standing water. Policies expect reasonable steps, and costs are usually covered.
  5. Keep receipts: Save every receipt for board-up, hotels, meals and supplies.
  6. Make a contents list: List damaged items, their age and approximate value.
  7. Get a repair estimate: Ask a restoration provider for a detailed, line-item estimate to compare with the adjuster's.

The USFA also warns homeowners to be careful with unsolicited contractors who arrive after a fire. Check licenses and get written estimates before signing anything.

Tennessee insurance rules that matter after a fire

Tennessee's Department of Commerce and Insurance offers clear guidance for claims after a loss:

Photograph damage before cleanup and keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, if it is safe. Take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Public adjusters must hold a Tennessee license, and they are paid a percentage of your settlement. Checks for contents usually go to the owner, while checks for the structure may include your mortgage company. Report any hidden damage you find later so it can be added to the claim.

Repairs also have rules. In Davidson and Rutherford counties, residential projects above $3,000 and under $25,000 need a contractor with a home improvement license. Any project of $25,000 or more needs a state contractor license. Metro Nashville Codes requires permits for work like wall removal, drywall over 100 square feet and roofing over one-third of the roof.

If you have a complaint about how a claim is being handled, TDCI's Consumer Insurance Services can help.

Fire claims in Nashville neighborhoods

Nashville's housing ranges from 1920s bungalows in East Nashville to large custom homes in Brentwood. Older homes may need code upgrades when walls are opened, so ordinance or law coverage matters more there. Larger homes have higher rebuild costs, so dwelling limits need regular review.

Storms are another Nashville factor. Tornadoes in 1998, 2020 and 2023 damaged homes across the metro. If your home had earlier storm damage that was never repaired, the adjuster may separate it from the fire claim. Keep records of past repairs.

How to lower your fire risk at home

  • Kitchen: Stay in the room when cooking. NFPA lists cooking as the leading cause of home fires.
  • Heating: Service furnaces each year and keep space heaters three feet from anything that can burn.
  • Dryers: Clean the lint filter after every load and the vent once a year.
  • Electrical: Replace damaged cords and have flickering lights or warm outlets checked.
  • Smoke alarms: Test monthly and replace alarms that are ten years old.

Local note: Coverage questions vary across the metro. Historic homes in Franklin and East Nashville may need ordinance or law coverage, large homes in Brentwood need high dwelling limits, and homes in Goodlettsville and Madison may still have storm damage that adjusters separate from fire damage.

  • The roof, windows or doors are open to weather after the fire.

  • Your adjuster needs a detailed repair estimate.

Homeowners insurance and fire damage questions

Will my homeowners insurance go up after a fire claim?

It may. Insurers consider claim history when setting rates, but an accidental fire is a common claim and the effect varies by company.

Can I choose my own restoration company?

Yes. You can choose your own provider. Your insurer may suggest companies, but the choice is yours.

Does homeowners insurance cover smoke damage from a neighbor's fire?

Usually yes. Smoke is typically a covered peril even when the fire started on another property. File with your own insurer.

What if I disagree with the adjuster's estimate?

Ask for a written explanation, provide your own contractor's estimate and request a reinspection. Many policies also include an appraisal process for disputes.

Does insurance cover a fire in a vacant house?

Coverage may be limited or excluded if the home was vacant longer than your policy allows. Check the vacancy clause before leaving a home empty.
