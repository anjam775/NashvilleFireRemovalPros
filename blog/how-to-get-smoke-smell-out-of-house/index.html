How to Get Smoke Smell Out of a House (After a Fire or Smoking)

By Nashville Fire Removal Pros Editorial · Published November 11, 2026 · Updated November 11, 2026 · 9 min read

Short answer: To get smoke smell out of a house, remove the source first. Air the house out, change the HVAC filter, then clean every surface the smoke touched, including walls, ceilings, floors, fabrics and ducts. Odor absorbers like baking soda and activated charcoal help after that. Air fresheners and candles only cover the smell for a while. Heavy fire smoke usually needs professional deodorizing.

A smoke smell can hang on for weeks after a small kitchen fire, and for months after a bigger one. It can also come from years of indoor smoking. Either way, the smell lives in tiny particles stuck to surfaces and soaked into soft materials. Why does smoke smell linger in a house?

Smoke smell lingers because smoke is made of tiny particles and gases that settle on every surface and sink into porous materials. Oklahoma State University Extension describes the odor as microscopic particles that cling to walls, furniture, floors and clothing.

Soft and porous things hold the most smell:

  • Fabrics: curtains, upholstery, carpet, bedding and clothes
  • Building materials: drywall, plaster, insulation and unsealed wood
  • Hidden spaces: ductwork, attics, closets and the insides of cabinets

Until those particles are cleaned away, the smell keeps coming back. That is why spraying an air freshener seems to work for an hour, then fails.

How to get smoke smell out of a house after a fire

The order matters. Cleaning in the wrong order can spread soot and set the smell. Use these steps for light to moderate smoke, after the fire department says the home is safe.

  1. Air the house out. Open windows and doors when the weather allows. Set box fans in windows to push air out, not in.
  2. Change the HVAC filter. Put in a new filter before you run the system. Texas A&M AgriLife Extension advises changing filters when you return and at least monthly for the first year.
  3. Remove soot before you wash. Vacuum loose soot with a HEPA vacuum, holding the nozzle just above the surface. Use a dry chemical sponge on walls before any water touches them.
  4. Wash hard surfaces. Clean walls, ceilings, floors, cabinets, shelves and the inside of drawers and closets with mild soap or a degreasing cleaner. Rinse and dry.
  5. Treat fabrics. Wash what you can. Have upholstery, rugs and curtains steam-cleaned or professionally cleaned.
  6. Set out odor absorbers. Leave bowls of baking soda or activated charcoal around the house for several days.
  7. Seal what still smells. After cleaning, prime stained or smelly walls with an odor-blocking primer before you repaint.
  8. Check again on a warm day. Heat and humidity release trapped odor. If the smell comes back, the source is still there.

Fire smoke vs. cigarette smoke: what changes

Both leave odor in the same places, but the residue is different. That changes how you clean.

Fire smoke Cigarette smoke
Residue Dry soot, sticky plastic residue or greasy protein film, depending on what burned Yellow-brown tar and nicotine film
Where it hides Everywhere the smoke traveled, often far from the fire Walls, ceilings, fabrics and ducts in rooms where people smoked
Biggest risk Wiping or washing too early, which smears soot deeper Painting over film, which lets stains bleed back through
What usually works Dry soot removal first, then washing, deodorizing and sealing Degreasing wash, then a stain- and odor-blocking primer
When to call a pro Smoke reached the HVAC system, attic or several rooms Years of heavy smoking or odor in carpet pad and drywall

How to get smoke smell out of walls and ceilings

Walls and ceilings are the biggest smoke surfaces in any house. Start dry with a soot sponge, then wash with a mild degreaser from the bottom up and rinse as you go. Seal with an odor-blocking primer once the walls are dry.

The step-by-step method is in the guide on how to clean smoke damage from walls and ceilings. Textured or popcorn ceilings should not be scrubbed. Older ones may contain asbestos and need testing first.

How to get smoke smell out of furniture

Furniture holds smell in fabric, cushions and wood grain. Vacuum fabric gently, sprinkle baking soda and let it sit, then steam-clean or have it professionally cleaned. Wood furniture can be wiped with a mild cleaner and aired out. Leather needs gentler care.

The full method for each material is in the guide on how to get smoke smell out of furniture.

How to get smoke smell out of clothes

Most smoky clothes can be saved with repeated washing. Oklahoma State University Extension suggests adding 1 cup of vinegar to the wash with your usual detergent. Rewash until the smell is gone, and do not put smoky clothes in the dryer, because heat can set the odor.

Read how to get smoke smell out of clothes for fabric-by-fabric steps.

How to get smoke smell out of carpet

Carpet holds smoke in its fibers and in the pad underneath. Start by vacuuming slowly with a HEPA vacuum, going over each area in two directions. Then sprinkle baking soda over the carpet, leave it overnight and vacuum it up.

If the smell stays, have the carpet cleaned with hot water extraction. Texas A&M AgriLife Extension lists steam or other suitable equipment for deodorizing carpets after smoke. Let the carpet dry fully, with fans and the air conditioner running, so it doesn't grow mildew.

Carpet that was soaked with sooty firefighting water is usually replaced, pad included. Soot and water work deep into the backing, and wet padding can grow mold within a day or two.

How to get rid of smoke smell in an apartment

In an apartment, smoke can come from your unit or from a neighbor's. It travels through shared walls, attics, hallways and vents.

  • Tell your landlord in writing. Smoke that comes through shared walls or vents is often a building problem, not just a cleaning problem.
  • Clean your own space. Wash walls, fabrics and hard surfaces the same way you would in a house.
  • Ask about the HVAC. In many apartments the landlord controls filters and ducts.
  • Check your renters policy. Smoke from a sudden fire is usually covered. The renters insurance guide explains what a policy pays for.
  • Keep records. Photos and dated notes help if the smell keeps coming back.

Do ozone generators remove smoke smell?

Ozone can break down some odors, but it is not safe to use in a home where people are present. The EPA says no federal agency has approved ozone generators for use in occupied spaces. It also notes that ozone can irritate the lungs, and that at levels within public health standards, ozone does little to remove many odor-causing chemicals.

Oklahoma State University Extension says homeowners should not run ozone generators themselves. A professional should run one, and the home has to be empty during treatment. People, pets and plants all need to be out of the space.

Restoration crews may also use hydroxyl generators or thermal fogging, depending on the job. These are best left to a trained provider.

Why smoke smell comes back in Nashville summers

Middle Tennessee summers are hot and humid. Warm, damp air makes porous materials like drywall, plaster and fabric release trapped smoke odor. A house that smells fine in February can smell smoky again in July.

Two things help. Keep indoor humidity under control with your air conditioner or a dehumidifier. Clean the HVAC system too, because ducts can hold smoke residue and blow it back into every room. Texas A&M AgriLife Extension recommends having HVAC units and ductwork professionally cleaned after smoke exposure.

Professionals follow industry standards such as ANSI/IICRC S700 for fire and smoke restoration. They have HEPA air scrubbers, hydroxyl generators and thermal foggers that most homeowners don't.

How to prevent smoke odor

  • Close doors during a fire. Closed doors slow the spread of smoke.
  • Keep HVAC filters fresh. A clean filter catches more particles.
  • Clean small smoke events right away. Burned food residue is easier to remove the first day than the first month.
  • Smoke outside. Indoor smoking leaves film on every surface over time.
  • Service heating equipment. A poorly tuned furnace can coat walls with soot.

Local note: Smoke odor acts differently around the metro. Lakeside homes in Hendersonville deal with extra humidity that brings odor back. Large homes in Brentwood often have several HVAC zones that spread smoke. Apartments in Antioch can take in smoke from a neighbor's unit.

Smoke smell removal questions

How long does smoke smell last in a house?

It can last for months or longer if the source is not removed. Odor stays in porous materials until they are cleaned, sealed or replaced. Airing out alone usually is not enough.

Does vinegar get rid of smoke smell?

It helps with light odor. Wiping hard surfaces with diluted white vinegar and leaving bowls of vinegar out can reduce smell. It will not remove heavy soot or odor soaked into drywall and insulation.

Can you paint over smoke smell?

Not with regular paint alone. Clean the surface first, then use a stain- and odor-blocking primer. Paint over dirty walls lets stains and smell bleed back through.

What is a furnace puff-back?

A puff-back happens when a furnace or boiler misfires and blows soot through the ducts and into the house. It often leaves a fine black film in many rooms at once and usually needs professional cleaning.

Does insurance pay for smoke odor removal?

Often, yes, when the smoke came from a sudden fire. Smoke from long-term smoking is usually not covered. Document the damage and ask your insurer before you hire anyone.
