Why fire damage in Goodlettsville and Madison is different

Much of Madison was built in the 1950s and 1960s, with brick and frame ranch homes on crawlspaces or slabs. Many still have original electrical panels, limited outlets and older heating systems. Homes with few outlets often rely on extension cords and space heaters. NFPA reports that space heaters account for most home heating fire deaths, and heating fires peak from December through February.

Manufactured homes are common in parts of both areas. They can burn quickly, and their thin wall panels and ceilings often hold smoke odor. Restoration may not be cost-effective after a serious fire, so insurers sometimes treat them as total losses. A provider can help document damage and secure the home while the adjuster decides.

Storms are part of the local story too. On December 9, 2023, an EF-2 tornado tore through Madison and continued northeast. Some roofs, windows and siding in the area were repaired after that storm, and any older damage can make a fire-damaged home harder to seal against rain.

Goodlettsville has a mix of older homes near its historic downtown and newer subdivisions off Long Hollow Pike. Because the city spans two counties, permit and license rules depend on which side of the line the address sits.

Tips for Goodlettsville and Madison homeowners

  • Space heaters: Plug space heaters directly into a wall outlet, keep them three feet from anything that can burn and turn them off when you leave the room.
  • Older panels: If breakers trip often or outlets feel warm, have a qualified electrician inspect the system.
  • Manufactured homes: Check whether your policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value, since it changes what a total loss pays.
  • Smoke alarms: The Get Alarmed, Tennessee! program provides free smoke alarms through many local fire departments.
  • Which county: Goodlettsville addresses can fall in Davidson or Sumner County, which affects permits and contractor license rules.

Permits and licensing in Goodlettsville and Madison

Madison is inside Metro Nashville, so permits come from Metro Codes. Goodlettsville runs its own codes office for addresses inside city limits. Davidson County requires a home improvement license for residential projects above $3,000 and under $25,000. Sumner County is not on that list. In both counties, projects of $25,000 or more need a state contractor license. Ask the provider which office and license apply to your address before work begins.

Fire damage restoration in Goodlettsville and Madison questions

Do providers cover all of Goodlettsville and Madison?

Yes. Providers in this network serve Madison zip code 37115 and Goodlettsville zip codes 37070 and 37072, including Rivergate, Gallatin Pike, Old Hickory Boulevard and Long Hollow Pike.

Is Madison part of Nashville?

Yes. Madison is a community inside Metro Nashville and Davidson County, so Metro Codes handles its permits.

Can a manufactured home be restored after a fire?

Light smoke damage can often be cleaned. Heavy fire damage may make replacement more practical, and insurers sometimes declare a total loss.

What causes most winter house fires here?

Heating equipment, especially space heaters, is a leading cause of winter home fires, along with cooking.

Which county is my Goodlettsville home in?

Goodlettsville spans Davidson and Sumner counties. Your property tax bill or the county assessor's site will show which county your address is in.
